# One — The Hatchery

The tour *begins* in a cold room. The Director of Hatcheries walks ahead of a flock of new students, and the students *write* down everything he says. Here, he tells them, human beings are no longer born. They are *decanted*. One egg is made to bud until it becomes ninety-six identical twins, and the twins are bottled, labelled and sent down a slow conveyor toward the light.

Henry Foster, who loves figures, *recites* them gladly: so many bottles, so many metres a day, so many workers for each of the five castes. Alphas are *made* to think. Epsilons are made to carry. Nobody is asked which they would rather be, because every one of them will be *taught* to like what they cannot escape. That, the Director says, is the whole secret of happiness.

Upstairs, in the nurseries, the teaching has already started. Infants *crawl* toward books and flowers, and then come the bells and the shocks. They will not crawl toward them twice. At night a quiet voice *repeats* the same sentences under every pillow, a hundred times, three nights a week, until the sentences are no longer something the child has heard. They are what the child *thinks*.

Lenina Crowne passes along the racks with her syringe, and Henry *smiles* at her across the bottles. Outside, in the garden, naked children *play* in the sun, and nothing in the world is wrong.

# Two — The Controller

Mustapha Mond arrives without warning, one of the ten men who run the world. He *tells* the students about the time before: homes, mothers, fathers, the tight hot rooms where people *loved* only a few and *suffered* for it. History, he says, was whatever could not be kept still. So the Controllers *chose*. They gave up truth and beauty, took comfort instead, and were thanked for it.

While he talks, the day shift ends. Lenina *tells* her friend Fanny that she has gone out with Henry for four months, and with nobody else. Fanny is shocked. Everyone *belongs* to everyone; it is hardly decent. Lenina agrees to try harder. She has been asked, as it happens, by Bernard Marx.

Bernard is an Alpha, but a short one, and people *whisper* that something went wrong in his bottle. He *hears* them. He *hates* the easy laughter of men like Henry, and hates even more that he *wants* what they have. His one friend is Helmholtz Watson, a writer of slogans who is too good at his work and has begun to *feel* that words could carry something heavier, if only he knew what.

When a feeling grows too sharp there is soma. One gramme and the evening *softens*; two, and the day never happened. Lenina *takes* hers without a thought. Bernard holds the tablet in his palm and, for as long as he can bear it, does not *swallow*.

# Three — The Reservation

Bernard *takes* Lenina to New Mexico, behind the fence where the old life has been left to go on. Before they leave, the Director *remembers* aloud, without meaning to, that he once went there too, with a girl, and *lost* her in a storm. Then he remembers himself, and *threatens* Bernard with Iceland.

The Reservation is dust, drums and old age. Lenina has never seen a wrinkled face and cannot *look* at one. She has forgotten her soma. In the square a boy is whipped until he falls, and a pale young man beside them *says* he wishes it had been him.

His name is John. His mother, Linda, came from the Other Place long ago, was hurt, and was left behind. She *bore* him here, to her lasting shame, and *taught* him to read from the only book she could find: an old volume of Shakespeare. The village never *accepted* either of them. John *grew* up between two worlds and belonged to neither.

Bernard *listens*, and *understands* exactly whose son this is. He *asks* John whether he would like to see London. John *looks* at Lenina, and *answers* with a line he has carried all his life, about a brave new world that has such people in it.

# Four — The Savage

In the Fertilizing Room, in front of all his workers, the Director *begins* to dismiss Bernard. Bernard *opens* the door. Linda *runs* to the Director with her arms wide; John kneels and *calls* him father. The room *laughs* until it cannot stand, and the Director is not seen there again.

Now everyone *wants* to meet the Savage, and so everyone must be kind to Bernard. He *enjoys* it more than he should. Helmholtz watches him enjoy it and *says* nothing.

John *walks* through the new world and likes it less each day: the identical faces at the factory gates, the feelies that touch the skin and never the mind, the tablets handed out like sweets. Linda has *asked* for soma and is given as much as she wants. She *sleeps* now, smiling, and will not wake for long.

He *loves* Lenina, and does not know how to *say* so except in verse. Lenina *wants* him too, in the only way she was ever taught to want anyone, and cannot *understand* why he will not simply take her hand. When at last she *offers* herself, he *shouts* and drives her away. Then the telephone *rings*. Linda is dying.

# Five — The Choice

At the hospital, children are brought to play between the beds so that they will *learn* not to mind death. John *weeps*, and they *stare* at him. When Linda is gone he *walks* out into a crowd of workers queuing for their soma and *throws* the boxes from the window, shouting that he has come to make them free. They do not *want* to be free. Helmholtz *runs* to help him, laughing. Bernard *hesitates* at the edge of the fight until the police arrive.

All three are *brought* before Mustapha Mond. The Controller has read Shakespeare too; he *keeps* the forbidden books in a safe. He *explains*, patiently, that tragedy needs instability, and that the world has paid for its peace with everything that used to be called great. Helmholtz and Bernard will be *sent* to islands, where those who think too much are allowed to live. Helmholtz *chooses* a cold one.

John *asks* for something else. He does not want comfort. He *wants* God, poetry, danger, freedom, goodness, sin. Mond *answers* that he is asking for the right to be unhappy. John *claims* it.

He *goes* alone to an abandoned lighthouse and tries to live clean. The cameras *find* him there, and after the cameras, the crowds. They *come* in helicopters to see the Savage, and one evening Lenina *comes* with them. In the morning the reporters push open the lighthouse door and *call* his name, and nobody *answers*.
